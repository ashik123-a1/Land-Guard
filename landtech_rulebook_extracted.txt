Part A: Rule Book
1 About LandTech 2026
1.1 Background
Land disputes are among the most common civil cases in Bangladesh. Many of them trace back
to forged deeds, conflicting khatians across survey generations (CS, SA, RS, BS), and records split
between the sub-registry oﬀice (registration) and the AC Land oﬀice (mutation). LandTech 2026,
the LandTech Innovation Hackathon, focuses on Land Tech Software: the foundational tools and
systems that make land management and e-governance work in production.
1.2 Track
Digital Land Records, Verification & Digitization
Build secure, tamper-proof systems and automated verification tools for land registries, deed
validation, and ownership transfers. This includes digitizing historical records (Khatian, Por-
cha, Deeds), preventing fraudulent transfers and double-selling, cryptographic validation, au-
tomated e-Mutation processing, and smart registry workflows.
1.3 Expected Outcomes
• High-quality projects.
• Industry-relevant innovations.
• Strong student engagement.
• Ideas that can be implemented countrywide.

3 Problem Statements
Each team must work on one of the following problem statements under the track and state it in
the concept note.
P1 — Historical Record Digitization
Convert scanned Khatian, Porcha, and Deed documents (handwritten and printed Bangla) into
structured, searchable data using Bangla OCR, data extraction, and validation.
P2 — Deed & Ownership Verification
Make land documents instantly verifiable using QR codes, digital signatures, hashing, or distributed
ledgers, so that a forged or altered copy is detected immediately.
P3 — Fraud & Double-Selling Detection
Detect forged documents, the same plot sold to multiple buyers, suspicious rapid transfers, and
mismatches between registration and mutation records.
P4 — Smart e-Mutation & Registry Workflow
Automate and link the flow from deed registration to mutation, with automated checks, status
tracking, and reduced manual intervention.
P5 — Citizen Pre-Purchase Verification
A “check before you buy” tool that lets citizens verify ownership, mutation history , pending cases,
and khas or acquisition status of a plot before a transaction.

4 Eligibility & Team Composition
1. The hackathon is open to current students and alumni of Daffodil International University
(DIU) only. Student members must provide a valid DIU student ID or proof of DIU alumni
status.
2. Each team consists of 4 members:
• 2 students (current DIU students or DIU alumni), one of whom is the Team Leader;
• 1 faculty mentor; and
• 1 industry mentor (a professional working in industry).
3. A student may be a member of only one team.
4. Mentors guide the team on the problem, design, and industry practice. The project must be
built by the student members, and the student members must present it.
5. T eam composition may not change after registration closes, except with written approval of the
organizers.
5 Registration & Fees
1. T eams register through the LandTech Hackathon Platform (landtech.diu.edu.bd/landtech) by
October 06, 2026 .
2. The Team Leader submits the team name, details of both students and both mentors, DIU ID or
alumni proof, and the chosen problem statement.
3. Registration fee (Round 1): 200 BDT per team, paid via bKash or Rocket at the time of regis-
tration.
4. Confirmation fee (Final Round): 100 BDT per team, payable only by teams selected for the
Final Round, within 3 days of the announcement of selected teams.
5. All fees are paid via bKash or Rocket and are non-refundable.
6. Registration is complete only after payment is verified and a confirmation is sent by the orga-
nizers.
7. All registration, submission, and status tracking is done on the LandTech Hackathon Platform.
8. Providing false information leads to disqualification at any stage.

8 Data, Privacy & Security Rules
These rules are strict. Any violation results in immediate disqualification and may be re-
ported to the relevant authorities.
1. T eams must use synthetic (dummy) data they create themselves, any sample data provided by
the organizers, or clearly public open data.
2. Real personal data — names, NID numbers, phone numbers, addresses, or actual land records
of real individuals — must not be collected, used, or displayed.
3. Scraping, automated querying, or bulk downloading from any government portal is prohibited.
4. Security testing, penetration testing, or any attempt to access a live government or third-party
system is strictly prohibited. Security features must be demonstrated only on the team’s own
prototype.
5. Any real document used for demonstration must be fully anonymized and approved by the
organizers in advance.
9 Use of AI Tools
1. AI coding assistants and large language models may be used.
2. All AI tools used must be disclosed in the README, with a short note on how they were used.
3. Both student members must be able to explain the submitted code and design. Judges may ask
either student technical questions; inability to explain the solution will affect scoring.
